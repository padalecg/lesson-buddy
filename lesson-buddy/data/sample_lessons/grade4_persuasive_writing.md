# Grade 4 ELA: Writing a Persuasive Letter

**Standards:** CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.4.1 (write opinion pieces supporting a point of view with reasons and information), CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.4.1.C (link opinion and reasons using words and phrases such as for instance, in order to, in addition).

**Duration:** 50 minutes (part 1 of a 3-day writing sequence)

## Objective

Students will be able to state a clear opinion and support it with at least two reasons, each backed by a fact or example, using linking words to connect ideas.

## Materials

- Mentor text: a sample student letter to the principal asking for a longer recess
- OREO graphic organizer (Opinion, Reason, Example, Opinion restated)
- List of linking words on an anchor chart
- Writing folders

## Hook (5 minutes)

Read two short sentences aloud: "We should have a class pet." and "We should have a class pet because caring for an animal teaches responsibility, and studies show pets help kids feel calmer." Ask: "Which one would convince the principal? Why?" Guide students to notice reasons and evidence.

## Mini-Lesson (15 minutes)

Introduce the OREO structure:

- **O**pinion: state what you believe clearly in the first sentence.
- **R**eason: give a reason why.
- **E**xample: support the reason with a fact, example or personal experience.
- **O**pinion restated: end by repeating your opinion in new words.

Read the mentor letter together and color-code each part with highlighters. Point out linking words: "for instance," "in addition," "another reason," and "as a result."

Common misconception: students often list many reasons with no support ("It's fun. It's good. Everyone wants it."). Emphasize that two well-supported reasons are stronger than five unsupported ones.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

As a class, choose a topic ("Our school should start a garden") and fill out an OREO organizer together on the board. Students suggest reasons; the class votes on which reasons are strongest and finds an example for each.

## Independent Writing (15 minutes)

Students choose their own topic from a menu (longer recess, a class pet, a reading corner, or their own idea approved by the teacher) and complete their own OREO organizer. Fast finishers begin drafting the letter's first paragraph.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide sentence starters: "I believe that ___. One reason is ___. For example, ___."
- **Extension:** Students add a counterargument ("Some people might say ___, but ___").
- **English language learners:** Allow students to brainstorm reasons orally or in their home language first, then translate key ideas with a partner.

## Assessment

Collect the OREO organizers. Check that each student has a clear opinion, two reasons, and an example supporting each reason. Use a simple checklist to group students for tomorrow's drafting conferences.
