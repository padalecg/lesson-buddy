# Grade 3 Math: Understanding Unit Fractions

**Standards:** CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.3.NF.A.1 (understand a fraction 1/b as one part when a whole is partitioned into b equal parts), CCSS.MATH.CONTENT.3.G.A.2 (partition shapes into parts with equal areas).

**Duration:** 55 minutes

## Objective

Students will be able to name and draw unit fractions (1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8) by partitioning a whole into equal parts, and explain why the parts must be equal.

## Materials

- Paper circles and rectangles (3 per student)
- Fraction strips
- Whiteboards and markers
- "Fair Share" anchor chart

## Hook (5 minutes)

Hold up a paper "pizza" cut into four pieces of very different sizes. Ask: "If I give you this piece, did you get one-fourth of the pizza?" Let students turn and talk. Guide them to the idea that fourths only count when all four pieces are the same size.

## Direct Instruction (15 minutes)

Introduce key vocabulary: **whole**, **equal parts**, **numerator**, **denominator**, and **unit fraction**. A unit fraction always has a numerator of 1. The denominator tells how many equal parts the whole is split into.

Model folding a rectangle in half, then in half again, to create fourths. Shade one part and write 1/4. Emphasize: "The bottom number counts the parts; the top number counts the parts we're talking about."

Common misconception to address: many students believe 1/8 is bigger than 1/4 because 8 is bigger than 4. Use fraction strips side by side to show that more equal parts means each part is smaller.

## Guided Practice (15 minutes)

In pairs, students fold paper circles into halves, fourths and eighths, labeling one part of each. Circulate and ask: "How do you know your parts are equal?" and "Which piece would you rather have if you were hungry, 1/2 or 1/8? Why?"

## Independent Practice (12 minutes)

Students complete the "Fair Share" worksheet: six shapes, some partitioned equally and some not. For each, they decide whether the shaded part shows a unit fraction and, if so, write it.

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Students who struggle use pre-folded shapes and only work with halves and fourths. Pair them with a peer partner during guided practice.
- **Extension:** Advanced students partition a rectangle into sixths two different ways and prove both show 1/6.
- **English language learners:** Provide a picture vocabulary card for whole, part, and equal, and allow sentence frames: "The whole is split into ___ equal parts. One part is ___."

## Assessment

Exit check: students draw a shape showing 1/3 and write one sentence explaining why their parts are equal. Collect whiteboards during the lesson to spot students who still choose 1/8 as larger than 1/4.
