# Grade 5 Science: How Plants Make Food

**Standards:** NGSS 5-LS1-1 (support an argument that plants get the materials they need for growth chiefly from air and water), NGSS 5-PS3-1 (use models to describe that energy in animals' food was once energy from the sun).

**Duration:** Two 45-minute sessions

## Objective

Students will be able to explain that plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide from the air to make their own food (sugar), and that most of a plant's mass comes from air and water, not soil.

## Materials

- Two bean plants: one kept in a dark closet for a week, one kept in sunlight
- Bag of potting soil and a kitchen scale
- Photosynthesis diagram handout
- Science notebooks

## Session 1: The Mystery of the Growing Tree (45 minutes)

**Hook:** Tell the story of Jan van Helmont, a scientist who in the 1600s planted a small willow tree in a pot of carefully weighed soil and watered it for five years. The tree gained about 74 kilograms, but the soil lost only a tiny amount. Ask: "Where did the tree's mass come from?"

Students write a prediction in their notebooks. Most will say "the soil." Record all predictions on chart paper without correcting them yet.

**Investigation:** Compare the two bean plants. Students observe and record color, height and leaf condition. Discuss: the plant in the dark is pale and weak even though it had the same soil and water. What was missing?

## Session 2: Building the Model (45 minutes)

**Direct instruction:** Introduce photosynthesis as a recipe. The ingredients are **carbon dioxide** (from the air, taken in through tiny holes in leaves), **water** (pulled up from the roots), and **energy from sunlight** (captured by green chlorophyll in the leaves). The products are **sugar**, which the plant uses for food and to build its body, and **oxygen**, which it releases into the air.

Return to van Helmont's tree. The tree's mass came mostly from carbon dioxide in the air and from water, built into sugar and then wood. Students revise their predictions in a different color.

Common misconception: students often think plants "eat" soil or get food from the ground. Clarify that soil provides small amounts of minerals, like vitamins, but not the bulk of the plant's food or mass.

**Energy connection:** Draw a simple food chain: sun → grass → rabbit → fox. Ask students to trace where the fox's energy originally came from (the sun, captured by the grass during photosynthesis).

## Differentiation

- **Support:** Provide a partially completed photosynthesis diagram where students fill in arrows and labels from a word bank.
- **Extension:** Students research why leaves change color in fall and connect it to chlorophyll.
- **English language learners:** Use the "recipe" frame with picture cards for sun, water, air, sugar and oxygen.

## Assessment

Students write a short argument (claim, evidence, reasoning) answering: "Where does most of a plant's mass come from?" They must use evidence from the bean plant observation or the van Helmont story.
